# Conor Neill — The Grown-Up Way to Open a Speech

## Summary

Short clip of Conor Neill making the case that adult speeches still run on the same engine as a bedtime story, just without the fairy-tale wrapper. Business speakers can't get away with "Once upon a time," so he demonstrates the grown-up equivalent: replace the fairy-tale frame with concrete specifics, then use a deliberate pause to hold the audience before delivering the payoff.

Source: clip from Conor Neill's full video *"How to Start a Speech"* (reposted by the Step Ahead channel).

---

## Decisions & insights

### 1. "Once upon a time" still works — it just needs adult clothing

He points out that figures like Jack Welch or Steve Jobs never literally open with "Once upon a time," but the underlying story structure is identical. The "grown-up" version swaps the fairy-tale marker for specific, verifiable detail: an exact month, an exact room, an exact headcount, a real person in conversation.

### 2. Specificity is what makes the opening credible

In his own demonstration, he anchors the story with concrete facts — the same room, months earlier, 120 people in attendance, a conversation with a named category of expert (a leading voice in public speaking) — before revealing what that person told him. The specificity does the work "Once upon a time" used to do: it signals "this really happened" and pulls the listener in.

### 3. The pause is a deliberate device, not a slip

He explicitly notes that he can hold silence for anywhere from 30 seconds to a couple of minutes at that point in the story. The pause is used to build anticipation for the payoff line before delivering it — treating silence as a tool with a defined purpose rather than dead air.

---

## Takeaway

Concrete, specific detail is the adult substitute for "Once upon a time" — it grounds the audience in a real moment and earns the right to a long pause before the payoff, which is what actually holds a room's attention.
