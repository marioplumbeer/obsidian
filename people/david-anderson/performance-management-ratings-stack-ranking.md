# Why Performance Management Works the Way It Does — Stack Ranking, PIPs, Ratings

## Summary
Dave Anderson (Scarlet Ink, ex-Amazon Tech Director/GM) argues that performance rating
systems — the parts employees only notice when something goes wrong — exist to serve two
deliberate functions: retaining top performers and managing out poor ones. The piece promises
to unpack Stack Ranking, PIPs, and ratings mechanics via a FAQ; only the opening rationale is
visible (the rest is paywalled).

## Decisions & insights

- **Two reasons ratings exist.** (1) Retention — top ratings are intentionally scarce and tied
  to higher compensation, so the best performers have a reason to stay rather than leave for a
  competitor. (2) Poor-performer management — ratings are the mechanism for identifying
  underperforming employees who should be separated from the company.

- **The manager bias problem.** Left uncontrolled, most managers would rate their entire team
  highly — they're biased toward people they work with daily and prefer a happy team. "Managers
  also don't like to hurt people's feelings" and would naturally avoid flagging poor performers.
  Capping the number of top ratings available forces managers to make real distinctions between
  performers instead of rating everyone well.

- **Why poor performers get identified at all.** Companies accept that bad hires are inevitable.
  Poor performers create real disruption — especially among top performers, who lose patience
  working alongside underperforming colleagues. Anderson's framing: removing underperformers is
  itself a retention tool for the people you actually want to keep.

- **Stack Ranking as the mechanism (paywalled).** The article introduces Stack Ranking as how
  companies operationalize identifying poor performers, but the mechanics, PIP process, ratings
  detail, and FAQ are behind the paywall and not captured here.
