# Denzel Washington — Preparing for Training Day With Real Gangsters and Cops

## Summary

Short clip of Denzel Washington describing how his preparation for *Training Day* was shaped by direct access to real Crips and Bloods, and to cops who had worked gang units, once Antoine Fuqua came on to direct. He describes the original script as closer to a conventional buddy-cop story, and credits that real-world access with hardening the character into what audiences saw on screen.

Source: [YouTube Short](https://youtube.com/shorts/nApxNz_US1g)

---

## Decisions & insights

### 1. The character grew out of real access, not just the page

The early script leaned closer to a standard buddy-cop tone. Once real gang members and gang-unit officers were brought in as consultants, that access — not just rewriting — is what he credits for turning the role into something harder and more specific.

### 2. "You have to be a gangster to deal with gangsters"

Gang-unit cops explained that survival in that world depends on nobody being sure who's informing on whom. Washington turned that logic into a piece of stagecraft for the character: he could bluff authority in front of a room of gang members just by implying he had informants inside, planting doubt without ever offering proof.

### 3. Authentic slang, not invented dialogue

Rather than approximating "gangster talk," he pulled specific terms directly from consultants:
- A former leader of the Athens Park Bloods explained real slang for a disciplinary housing unit — which Washington initially misheard as being about footwear.
- Consultants gave him the actual street term for smoking PCP, tied to its physical effect, which he worked into a scene opposite Ethan Hawke's character.

His method: keep asking practitioners what they actually call something, then build the character's choices around their literal answer instead of a generic approximation.

---

## Takeaway

Washington treated real practitioners as source material for the performance itself — borrowing their exact language and unwritten survival logic so the character's authority and menace felt earned rather than acted.
