# Emotional Suppression and the Brain's Decision-Making "Council"

## Summary
Dr. Alok Kanojia ("Dr. K"), a Harvard-trained psychiatrist and co-founder of [Healthy Gamer](https://www.healthygamer.gg/dr-alok-kanojia), explains why suppressing emotions doesn't actually remove them from decision-making — it just pushes them underground, where they still bias your choices without your awareness.

Source: [HealthyGamerGG (YouTube Shorts)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xZBuKavD1ss)

---

## Decisions & insights

### 1. High-IQ people suppress emotions — they don't erase them
People with high IQ tend to lean on emotional suppression as a coping tactic, becoming "hyper-rational." But suppressing an emotion doesn't turn it off — it just stops it from entering conscious deliberation. The amygdala (where anxiety is processed) still connects to the anterior cingulate cortex and frontal lobes — the brain's actual decision-making apparatus — whether or not you're consciously acknowledging the anxiety.

### 2. The brain as a "council" — three voices in every decision
Dr. K frames the decision-making apparatus as a council: the amygdala raises alarm ("we're anxious about this"), the nucleus accumbens (dopaminergic reward circuitry) pushes for what feels good now, and the frontal lobes act as the deliberating "head of the council," weighing both inputs before acting. His example: responding to a text from an ex — the amygdala warns it will go badly, the nucleus accumbens argues for the short-term reward, and the frontal lobes referee the fight.

> "Amygdala comes in and says, 'We're anxious about this.' Nucleus accumbens dopaminergic circuitry comes in and says, 'But yeah, but we really want it.' And then they fight."

### 3. Suppression doesn't remove a vote — it just hides it from the council
When you suppress an emotion, the anxious signal isn't deleted from the process — it's excluded from conscious deliberation ("the council chamber") while still wired into other circuits, including the reward system. It keeps shaping subconscious calculations without your knowledge, meaning suppression doesn't produce clearer decisions — it produces decisions you can't fully explain to yourself.

### 4. The fix for high performers isn't "be smarter" — it's "feel more"
For someone highly capable but underperforming, the answer usually isn't more intellect — it's more emotional contact. Dr. K's prescription: first, understand what you're actually feeling; second, accept that dealing with emotions directly (not suppressing them) is non-negotiable for good decision-making.

> "The solution isn't that you need to be smarter... The solution is that you need to be more in touch with your emotions."
