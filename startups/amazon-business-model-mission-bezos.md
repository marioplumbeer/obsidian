# Founder Jeff Bezos Discusses Amazon's Business Model & Mission

## Summary
Jeff Bezos lays out Amazon's core mission — to be "Earth's most customer-centric company" — and the business logic behind it: relentless focus on selection, price, and delivery, funded by a willingness to run new businesses at zero or negative impact to financials for five to seven years. The through-line is that customer obsession isn't a values statement bolted onto the business; in Bezos's framing, it *is* the strategy, and financial performance is a downstream outcome of it rather than a competing priority.

Source: [Founder Jeff Bezos discusses Amazon Business Model Mission](https://youtu.be/mk0qTPqaFcw)

---

## Decisions & insights

### The mission is the strategy, not a slogan
Bezos states Amazon's mission as being "Earth's most customer-centric company." He frames the company's financial health as an *outcome* of customer obsession, not a separate goal being traded off against it — investment decisions get filtered through "does this serve the customer" first.

### Three things retail has to get right
Bezos names selection, low prices, and fast/convenient/reliable delivery as the three critical elements of the retail business. He argues Amazon's online model gives it structural cost advantages over physical retail that let it sustain lower prices while also carrying much broader selection than any store could stock.

### Long horizons by design
New Amazon businesses are expected to have no financial impact — or a negative one — for their first five to seven years. This isn't treated as a risk to manage around; it's built into how Amazon evaluates new bets, which only works if leadership (and investors) are willing to be misunderstood for a long time before the payoff shows up.

### Willingness to be misunderstood
Bezos's 1997 shareholder promise to "focus relentlessly on our customers" was made while skeptics doubted Amazon's viability. The pattern recurs: a long-term, customer-first bet looks irrational or unprofitable by short-term financial metrics until it isn't.

---

## Key takeaways
1. Customer obsession functions best as a decision filter for capital allocation, not just a values statement — it should change what gets funded and for how long.
2. A durable low-price, high-selection position comes from structural cost advantages (in Amazon's case, the online model), not just discounting.
3. Multi-year, deliberately unprofitable investment horizons (5-7 years) can be a genuine strategic choice rather than a failure mode, provided the long-term thesis is sound.
4. Being willing to look wrong by conventional short-term metrics is often the cost of pursuing a long-term customer-first bet.
