# How to Get Your First Customers (Gustaf Alströmer, YC Startup School)

## Summary
Gustaf Alströmer (YC Group Partner; former Head of Growth/Product Lead at Airbnb) gives tactical advice on acquiring a startup's earliest customers, for both B2B and B2C companies. Core message: in the beginning, sales doesn't scale and shouldn't — founders themselves must manually recruit customers one conversation at a time, pick the easiest customers first, and instrument the process like a funnel so drop-off is visible early.

Source: [How to Get Your First Customers — YC Startup School](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hyYCn_kAngI)

---

## Decisions & insights

### 1. Do things that don't scale
A good product is rarely built in isolation — it's built together with your customers. Startups don't take off on their own; founders make them take off by manually recruiting customers, one at a time, in a way that clearly won't scale (and shouldn't need to, yet). The unscalable work itself is the mechanism that produces the product feedback and traction that make scaling later possible.

### 2. Founder-led sales
In the early stage, the founders — not a hired salesperson — should be the ones doing sales. Founders are the only ones who can absorb raw customer feedback fast enough to change the product in response, and outsourcing that loop away from the founding team removes the signal it's meant to produce.

### 3. Start with your easiest customers, not your hardest
Sales efficiency early on comes from picking the customers who are easiest to close, not the most ambitious or prestigious ones. This is not the time to go after the hardest sale first — chasing a marquee logo before the product and pitch are proven wastes the scarce early cycles that should go toward learning and iteration.

### 4. Instrument the funnel
Track each step of the sales process (outreach → response → demo → close, or the equivalent for a given business) so drop-off points are visible rather than assumed. Alströmer recommends simple, lightweight CRM tooling — tools like Apollo.io or Pipedrive — over building custom tracking, since the goal at this stage is visibility, not sophistication.

### 5. Growth channels are where people already look
Channels are just the platforms where people already tend to discover products — Google being one of the largest for new-product discovery. Direct/personal outreach, email, and referral mechanics round out the early acquisition toolkit; the point isn't to invent a new discovery surface but to show up on the ones that already exist.

### 6. The same playbook works for B2B and B2C
The tactics — founder-led selling, prioritizing easy wins, funnel visibility — aren't specific to enterprise sales; the same discipline applies whether the "customer" is a consumer signing up or a business buyer signing a contract.

---

## Key takeaways
1. Manually, personally recruiting your first customers is expected and necessary — not a sign you haven't found product-market fit yet.
2. Founders themselves should run early sales; the direct feedback loop is the point.
3. Chase easy customers first; save the hard, prestigious ones for later.
4. Track the funnel with simple CRM tools so you can see where prospects actually drop off.
5. Growth channels are existing discovery surfaces (search, email, referrals) — not something to invent from scratch.
