# Losing your accent

## Summary
Paul Thompson traces his path from dismissing the humanities in favor of "exact" subjects like math and programming, to realizing that persuasive/technical writing is just as core to his engineering career. He argues that AI can produce flawless, "accentless" writing, but that the quirks, imperfections, and personal voice — the writing "accent" — are what make writing recognizably human and worth keeping.

Source: [Losing your accent, Paul Thompson](https://paulthompson.dev/posts/accent/)

---

## Key ideas

### 1. From "exact" subjects to valuing writing
Thompson loved math, science, and programming in school because they were provably correct — a bad grade was your own fault. He initially wrote off English, even bullshitting his way to one of his best marks and bombing after actually studying. Years into a software engineering career, he's come to see basic proficiency in writing — expressing ideas persuasively, not just building the solution but convincing others it has legs — as just as important as technical skill.

### 2. Writing has an "accent"
Thompson calls his own distinctive voice — his turns of phrase, love of alliteration, in-jokes and book references, and (self-admittedly wrong) comma usage — his writing "accent." It's not tied to geography; it's the thing that, after enough exposure, makes the author's name on a piece redundant to a regular reader.

### 3. AI produces "accentless" writing, and that's the problem
He describes more and more writing as "accentless" — precise, porcelain-smooth, jokes that feel committee-approved. He'd rather read a bad joke that didn't land, because the "gracelessness that comes with failure is human." Polished-but-generic writing makes authors blend together and lose individual identity.

> "Lean into your humanness, and be honest when you want to achieve it."

### 4. The ask: keep your accent
He accepts AI can produce a clear PRD, but says if he's going to "fly your flag," he needs to see the human behind it — preferences, imperfect grammar, a love of bullet points, real passion for the subject. His closing line: keep your accent when you write, the same way you have one when you speak.

> "What do you call a fish with no eyes? ... Fsh."
